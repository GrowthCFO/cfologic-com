E-Invoicing Mandates 2026–2028: A Timeline for Multi-Country Finance Teams

By the CFOLogic team

An issue of AI for CFOs, a weekly newsletter. Subscribe →

Idea in brief

A dated timeline of e-invoicing mandates from official sources as of 21 October 2026 (EU ViDA, Belgium, Poland, Germany, France, Croatia, Spain's VERI*FACTU billing-software rules, India's 30-day reporting limit and Mexico's CFDI 4.0), what each changes inside AP and AR, and a checklist for multi-country finance teams.

Which e-invoicing mandates are already in effect. As of 21 October 2026: Belgium, Croatia for VAT-registered taxpayers, Poland's KSeF for all taxpayers, France for receiving and for large and mid-sized issuers, Germany's receiving requirement, India's 30-day reporting limit and Mexico's CFDI 4.0.
What changes on 1 January 2027. Poland's small-invoice relief and Germany's general transition both end on 31 December 2026, Croatia's non-VAT taxpayers start issuing, and Spain's VERI*FACTU deadline for companies falls, subject to further change.
What comes in 2027 and 2028. French SMEs and micro-enterprises issue e-invoices from 1 September 2027, Germany's rules apply to all from 1 January 2028, and the EU's ViDA platform and single VAT registration measures apply from 1 July 2028.
What a finance team should do now. Confirm scope entity by entity, decide the sending and receiving route per country, clean customer and supplier master data, and add rejection and clearance status to the AP and AR exception queues.

Picture the invoice run at a group with entities in Poland, France and Germany. In Poland, invoices now go through the national KSeF system. In France, since 1 September, every business must be able to receive e-invoices, and large and mid-sized companies must issue them. In Germany, the general transition that allowed other invoice formats ends on 31 December 2026, with a narrower one for 2027. One accounts receivable team, three rule sets, three different sets of failure points.

E-invoicing mandates used to be a question for the tax team and the ERP vendor. They now change an invoice's format, its route and, in some countries, how quickly it must be reported. This guide sets out the dates from official sources as they stood on 21 October 2026, and what to do before the next cluster of deadlines on 1 January 2027.

Country notes and caveats

EU. Directive (EU) 2025/516 was adopted on 11 March 2025 and entered into force on 14 April 2025, which lets Member States introduce mandatory e-invoicing. Expect more national mandates before the 2030 cross-border reporting date, each on its own timetable.

Poland. The low-value relief ends on 31 December 2026, so entities that relied on it need their full KSeF process working from January.

Germany. The transitional rules sit in § 27(38) of the VAT Act. Each German entity should test its own prior-year turnover against the €800,000 threshold for 2027 supplies, and note that EDI is a separate route.

Spain (VERI*FACTU). These rules govern billing software, not the exchange of B2B e-invoices, so treat them as a systems compliance project. The dates come from RDL 15/2025 as set out in an AEAT note updated 13 October 2026. They are subject to further change; check the AEAT notice before planning around them.

India. The 30-day limit turns a reporting backlog into a compliance failure. AR has to report on a short, regular cycle, so a late batch or a stuck integration does not push invoices past the limit.

Mexico. CFDI 4.0 has been mandatory since 2023. For groups with Mexican entities, the work is data quality in each CFDI rather than adoption.

What changes inside the finance process

E-invoicing is often scoped as an IT integration. Most of the work lands on AP and AR.

Master data. Structured invoices fail validation on tax IDs, addresses and legal names that a PDF tolerated. Clean customer and supplier records before go-live.

A new exception type. Rejections become a daily queue. Someone has to own them, with a time limit, or invoices sit unissued while cash waits.

Receiving is a process too. AP must accept structured invoices through the required channel and match them to orders and receipts. Germany required receiving from 2025, well before issuing, and in France every business must receive a year before SMEs must issue.

Credit notes and timing. Corrections often follow the same route as invoices, and India's rule shows that some regimes care about speed as well as format.

Where AI helps, and where it does not

Rejections. AI can read a rejection message, identify the likely cause, such as a missing tax ID or a mismatched total, and draft the fix for a person to approve.

Matching received invoices. Structured data makes PO and receipt match suggestions more reliable than reading PDFs. The usual three-way match rules still apply.

Watching sources. A model can check official pages for changes and summarise them for the person who owns the calendar. That person verifies against the source before changing anything.

What AI should not do: decide whether an entity is in scope, interpret a rule, or issue invoices without a person owning the process. A misread threshold has a real cost, and a confident summary of a rule that has since changed is worse than none.

What an e-invoicing project will not fix

It will not fix the invoice content. A structured invoice with the wrong price, the wrong customer or the wrong tax treatment is still wrong, only delivered faster.

It will not settle tax positions. Whether a transaction is in scope, which rate applies and how a cross-border supply is treated are questions for tax advisers in each country. The e-invoicing route only carries the answer.

Frequently asked questions

Which countries have e-invoicing mandates in effect in 2026?

As of 21 October 2026, mandates are in effect in Belgium, Croatia for VAT-registered taxpayers, Poland and France for receiving and for large and mid-sized issuers. Germany has required businesses to receive e-invoices since 1 January 2025, and India's 30-day reporting limit and Mexico's CFDI 4.0 also apply.

When does e-invoicing become mandatory for all businesses in Germany?

From 1 January 2028. Invoices other than e-invoices are allowed for 2025 and 2026 supplies until 31 December 2026, and for 2027 supplies only where prior-year turnover was not more than €800,000 or the invoice is exchanged by EDI.

When do French SMEs have to issue e-invoices?

From 1 September 2027. Since 1 September 2026 all French businesses must be able to receive e-invoices, and large and mid-sized companies must issue them.

Is Spain's VERI*FACTU an e-invoicing mandate?

No, VERI*FACTU sets rules for billing software rather than for exchanging B2B e-invoices. Companies must adapt before 1 January 2027 and other taxpayers before 1 July 2027, subject to further change.

What is India's 30-day e-invoice reporting rule?

Since 1 April 2025, taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover of ₹10 crore and above cannot report e-invoices, credit notes or debit notes older than 30 days on the invoice registration portal.
