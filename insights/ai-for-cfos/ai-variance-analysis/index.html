AI Variance Analysis: Commentary a Board Can Trust

By the CFOLogic team

An issue of AI for CFOs, a weekly newsletter. Subscribe →

Idea in brief

How to use AI variance analysis for board and management commentary without losing trust: keep every figure sourced from the ledger, label each explanation by the evidence behind it, set materiality thresholds in advance, and give a named reviewer the final word, with what the research says about early impact and assurance readiness.

What AI variance analysis is. AI variance analysis uses software to calculate movements against budget or prior periods, find the transactions behind them and draft plain-language commentary for a person to review.
Where it goes wrong. In the explanation rather than the arithmetic: a plausible cause the model inferred from an account name, a timing difference presented as performance, or offsetting movements it never mentions.
How to make it trustworthy. Take every figure from the ledger, label each explanation by its evidence, set materiality thresholds before the month starts, and have a named reviewer sign off.
What stays with management. Deciding what the variances mean for the plan and what to do about them, which is the part of the commentary a board actually reads for.

Take a board pack due out on Thursday. On Wednesday evening the CFO reads the variance commentary and stops at a line saying marketing spend was over budget because of a trade show. The figure is right. The trade show was last quarter, and the overspend came from an agency retainer that started early. The wrong sentence reads exactly as confidently as the correct ones around it.

That is the central risk in AI variance analysis. The arithmetic is rarely the problem. The explanation is, and a board that finds one invented cause will start to doubt the rest of the pack.

What AI variance analysis does reliably

Most of the work in variance commentary is mechanical, and software does it well. It calculates every movement against budget, forecast and prior year. It ranks them against the thresholds you set. It pulls the journal lines, vendors and customers behind each material movement, and it notices patterns a tired analyst skips, such as a cost line that has crept up for five months without crossing the threshold in any single one.

Finance teams have been moving this way for a while. Gartner's release of 18 November 2025, based on a May–June 2025 poll of 183 CFOs and senior finance leaders, listed error and anomaly detection (34%) among the leading finance AI use cases, after knowledge management (49%) and AP process automation (37%). It also found "91% of respondents reporting low or moderate impact initially", which argues for putting the controls in place before the volume.

Figures from the ledger, words from the model

The first rule is architectural. The model should never produce a number. Figures come from the ledger or the planning system by query, and the model receives them and writes about them. Every amount and percentage in the commentary should trace back to a source line someone can open.

This also makes checking practical. If the draft says revenue was 4% below budget, an automated tie-out can compare that figure with the variance table before a person reads a word. Any number in the text that does not match the table is a defect, and the draft goes back. Our earlier piece on the month-end close covers the same principle for close commentary.

Checking the explanations

Errors in AI-drafted commentary tend to fall into a few types. The model infers a cause from an account name. It presents a timing difference as performance. It describes one large movement and misses the offsetting one that makes the net figure small. Or it carries over last month's explanation because the pattern looks the same.

A simple discipline is to label each explanation by the evidence behind it.

The last row is the one to watch. An unconfirmed explanation is a question for the budget owner, not a sentence for the board. For how to turn confirmed movements into a story an audience can act on, see From Data to Decisions: Finding the Story in the Numbers.

Materiality thresholds, set in advance

AI will write about whatever it is pointed at. If every movement gets a paragraph, the board reads none of them. Agree thresholds before the month starts, combining a percentage and an amount so that small lines do not dominate, and set them per section where the business needs it. Document them, and let the audit committee or board see what they are.

Below the threshold, use AI for what it does well: trends. Three small adverse movements in the same direction can matter more than one large favorable one, and a person scanning a long variance report may not spot them.

A reviewer, by name

Assurance is where many teams are behind. When KPMG surveyed 1,013 senior finance leaders in March 2026, only 42 percent of organizations were "fully assurance-ready" when it came to AI-enabled finance processes.

Commentary that goes to a board needs one person who has reviewed it and answers for it. That person checks that the figures tie, that every explanation has its evidence, and that the overall message matches what management believes. Record the sign-off with the date and the version reviewed, so the trail survives a question from the audit committee months later.

What AI variance analysis will not tell you

No variance report says what to do. It explains the past month. What it means for hiring, pricing or the forecast is a management judgment, and the commentary should state it as one.

Nor will it tell you whether the budget was realistic. A large variance against a budget nobody believed says more about the budget than the business. AI will explain the gap faithfully either way, so someone has to say which kind of gap it is.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI variance analysis?

AI variance analysis uses software to calculate movements against budget, forecast or prior periods, identify the transactions behind them and draft plain-language commentary for a finance professional to review and approve.

Can AI write board variance commentary?

AI can write a good first draft, provided the figures come from the ledger and a named reviewer confirms each explanation before the commentary goes to the board. It should not be the final author.

How do you stop AI from inventing explanations?

Require evidence for every explanation, such as journal entries, operational data or a confirmation from the budget owner, and treat any cause inferred only from an account name or past pattern as a question rather than a finding.

What materiality threshold should variance commentary use?

Use a threshold that combines a percentage and an amount, agreed before the period starts and documented, so that commentary focuses on movements that matter and small lines with large percentage swings do not crowd them out.

Who should review AI-generated variance commentary?

A named finance professional who understands the business, usually the FP&A lead or controller, with the CFO accountable for anything that goes to the board. The review should be recorded.
