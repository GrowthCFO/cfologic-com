Duplicate Payments: How They Happen and the Controls That Catch Them

By the CFOLogic team

Idea in brief

How duplicate payments happen in accounts payable (re-keyed invoices, duplicate vendor records, reference format variations, credit memo errors and manual payments outside the run), the difference between preventive and detective controls, how fuzzy matching works and where it produces false alarms, and how to recover and root-cause a duplicate once found.

How duplicate payments happen. The same invoice is keyed twice with small differences, posted to two records for one vendor, paid manually and then again in the run, or a credit memo is mishandled.
Why the ERP check misses them. Standard duplicate checks compare a fixed set of fields for an exact match, so a reference keyed as INV-0042 one day and 42 the next passes.
Preventive versus detective. Preventive controls stop the second posting; detective controls find it before or after the money leaves. A sound AP function needs both, and the most valuable window is the review of the payment proposal.
What fuzzy matching and AI add. They score near-matches across reference, amount, date, vendor and bank account, and explain why two invoices look alike. A person decides whether to hold the payment.

A supplier sends a statement showing a credit balance on your account, and nobody in AP can say where it came from. Tracing it takes an afternoon: the same invoice arrived by email and by post, was keyed twice a week apart, and was paid in two runs. This vendor flagged it. Many duplicate payments are never flagged and sit as credits in a supplier's ledger until someone asks.

Most ERPs check for duplicate invoices. Duplicates still get through, because the second copy rarely looks exactly like the first.

How duplicate payments happen

Re-keyed invoices. The same invoice arrives through two channels, or a reminder copy arrives after the original. It is keyed again with a slightly different reference, a different invoice date or a rounded amount.

Duplicate vendor records. One supplier exists twice in the vendor master, perhaps after an acquisition, a change of name or a new remit-to address. The duplicate check runs within a vendor, so an invoice posted to each record passes.

Reference format variations. INV-0042, INV0042, 0042 and 42 are the same invoice to a person and four different invoices to an exact-match check. Dates keyed in day-month and month-day order cause the same problem.

Credit memos and reversals. An invoice is reversed and re-posted, and the original payment is not matched to the new document. Or a credit memo is entered as an invoice and paid.

Manual payments outside the run. An urgent wire goes out for a supplier who is threatening to stop deliveries. The invoice is later posted in the normal way, nothing links it to the manual payment, and the run pays it again.

We have not found a published duplicate-payment rate we would cite with confidence. The closest broad measure is APQC Open Standards Benchmarking (cross-industry median, all companies): 95.0% of disbursements are first time error free, across 2,479 companies. That covers every kind of disbursement error, not duplicates alone.

Preventive and detective controls for duplicate payments

A preventive control stops the second invoice from being posted or paid. A detective control finds it afterwards. Both matter, and the most useful detective control runs before the money leaves.

Two settings are worth checking this week. One is whether the ERP's duplicate check stops the posting or only warns; a warning AP clicks through daily is a weak control. The other is which fields it compares, a choice the controller should document.

Fuzzy matching: what it adds and where it misfires

Fuzzy matching compares invoices on how alike they are, not whether they are identical. A typical approach normalises the reference (stripping prefixes, spaces and leading zeros), then scores pairs on reference similarity, amount, invoice date, vendor name and bank account. Pairs above a threshold go to a reviewer.

It produces false alarms. Monthly rent, retainers and instalments repeat the same amount by design. Treat recurring payments as a separate class with their own expected schedule, or the review list fills with noise and people stop reading it.

AI has a place here. Error and anomaly detection was among the top AI use cases, at 34%, in Gartner's May and June 2025 survey (183 CFOs and senior finance leaders). In payables, a model can compare the invoice documents, line items included, and say plainly why two look like one: same PO, same delivery note, references differing only by a prefix. It can also link a manual payment to the invoice it settled.

It should not stop a payment on its own. A flagged pair goes to a person who checks the documents and decides whether to hold. Log every flag and every decision, so the controller can see how often the flags are right.

Recovering a duplicate and fixing the cause

When a duplicate is found after payment, ask the vendor for a refund or agree an offset against the next invoice, in writing. Then trace which control failed: a vendor record that should have been merged, a manual payment never linked, a check set to warn. Fix the control as well as the transaction.

Reconciling statements from larger suppliers finds credits they have not mentioned. It is dull work and the last line of defence.

What duplicate payment controls will not fix

They will not catch a payment that is designed to look different. An invoice from a fictitious vendor, or a genuine invoice paid to a changed bank account, is not a duplicate. That is a vendor master problem and needs its own controls.

They will not make up for a vendor master that is already full of duplicate records. Matching on tax ID and bank account helps, but cleaning the master is a separate piece of work that someone has to own.

Frequently asked questions

What causes duplicate payments in accounts payable?

Duplicate payments are usually caused by the same invoice being keyed twice with small differences, by duplicate vendor records, by manual payments that are not linked to the invoice, or by mishandled credit memos and reversals.

Why does the ERP's duplicate invoice check not stop every duplicate?

It does not, because it looks for an exact match on a fixed set of fields. A reference keyed differently or a second vendor record is enough to pass it.

What is the difference between preventive and detective controls for duplicate payments?

A preventive control stops a duplicate invoice from being posted or paid, while a detective control finds it afterwards. The most useful detective control reviews the payment proposal for near-matches before the money leaves.

What is fuzzy matching for duplicate invoices?

Fuzzy matching scores pairs of invoices on how similar they are across reference, amount, date, vendor and bank account, rather than requiring an exact match. Recurring payments need separate treatment or they produce false alarms.

How do you recover a duplicate payment?

Ask the vendor for a refund or agree an offset against the next invoice, and confirm it in writing. Then trace which control failed and fix it.
