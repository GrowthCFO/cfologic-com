Fractional CFO Services: Cost and When You Need One

By the CFOLogic team

Idea in brief

What fractional CFO services cost in 2026, what the role does in the first 90 days, when to hire one, and the execution gap that derails most engagements.

What it is. A fractional CFO is a senior finance leader working part-time hours, usually five to twenty a week. That is a staffing arrangement, not a service category. What you get depends on the scope you write and on the capacity sitting beneath the role.
What it costs. The median fractional CFO rate is $175 an hour, with most engagements between $125 and $220 (Go Fractional, 2026). At ten hours a week that is roughly $63,000 to $110,000 a year, against a full-time CFO base salary range of $195,500 to $321,750 (Robert Half, 2026).
When to hire one. Revenue is a weak trigger. Events are reliable ones — a raise, a covenant, an acquisition, a margin move nobody can explain, or a decision you cannot model.
Where it goes wrong. Not price. The common failure is buying judgment without buying the capacity to execute it, and then wondering why the forecast still lands three weeks late.

Founders call us with a version of the same problem: four months into a fractional CFO engagement, and the CFO is good — twenty-five years, two exits, sharp on unit economics. The complaint is never about them. It is that every meeting produces a list of things that need to be built — a rolling forecast, a proper revenue schedule, a cash model the bank will accept — and there is nobody to build any of it. The CFO has eight hours a week. Six go to the meetings.

That is the shape of most fractional CFO disappointments. Not a bad hire. A structure that was never going to work.

This is a guide to buying the arrangement properly: what a fractional CFO is, what the market charges, when the role earns its cost, and what it will not fix on its own.

What a fractional CFO actually is

A fractional CFO is an experienced finance executive who works for your company on part-time or defined-scope hours, usually somewhere between five and twenty a week, usually across two to five clients at once.

The title covers a wide range of people. Some are former public-company CFOs. Some are controllers who have taken on the label. The market has grown fast enough that the title alone tells you almost nothing, so scope and references do the work that the title cannot.

The role is best understood by what it owns rather than what it is called.

RoleTypical hoursWhat it ownsCommon trigger
BookkeeperPart-timeTransaction recording, bank reconciliations, AP/AR entryBusiness has revenue
ControllerPart-time to full-timeClose calendar, accounting policy, controls, accuracy of the numbersClose is late or wrong
Fractional CFO5–20 hrs/weekForecast, capital strategy, pricing and margin decisions, board and lender relationshipsA decision or an event the founder cannot model alone
Interim CFOFull-time, fixed termEverything a full-time CFO owns, for a defined periodSudden departure, transaction, turnaround
Full-time CFOFull-timeAll of the above, permanently, plus building the teamComplexity is continuous, not episodic

Two distinctions matter more than the rest.

Fractional is not interim. Interim is a full-time seat filled temporarily, usually after a resignation or during a transaction. Fractional is a permanent arrangement at part-time hours. Companies conflate them and then hire the wrong one — an interim CFO for an ongoing capability gap, or a fractional CFO for a fourteen-hour-a-day crisis.

Fractional is not senior bookkeeping. If the actual problem is that the books close on day twenty-five and half the accruals are guesses, a CFO will diagnose that in week one and then need someone else to fix it. We wrote about that gap in Your Business Outgrew Your Bookkeeper Two Years Ago.

Why demand moved

The shift is real and it shows up in the hiring data, not just in vendor marketing.

Russell Reynolds' Q1 2026 Global CFO Turnover Index found that interim CFO appointments made up 12% of new CFO hires in the first quarter of 2026, up from 6% across 2025. That is the large-cap end of the market, but it points at the same thing SMBs are doing: companies have become comfortable putting a senior finance leader in place on a basis other than a permanent full-time seat.

Business Talent Group, part of Heidrick & Struggles, reported interim finance-chief requests rising 103% year over year in 2023 — the number that has been recycled across the internet ever since. It is real, it is specific, and it is now three years old. Treat it as the start of the trend rather than a description of today.

Underneath the executive-search data, the drivers for a $3–30M business are simpler:

Full-time CFO compensation has kept climbing. Robert Half's 2026 guide puts the national CFO salary range at $195,500 to $321,750, with a midpoint of $269,750 — base only, before bonus, equity, benefits and payroll tax.
Financial managers, the senior layer below CFO, had a median wage of $166,570 as of May 2025, and the BLS projects that occupation to grow 10% between 2025 and 2035, roughly three times the average for all occupations. Senior finance talent is getting more expensive to hold, not less.
Remote work made it normal for a finance leader to run a company they have never physically visited.

None of that is a fashion. Most companies between $3M and $30M in revenue have CFO-grade problems for perhaps a day a week, and cannot justify a CFO-grade salary for five days of it.

What a fractional CFO should be doing

A good engagement front-loads diagnosis, then settles into a rhythm. If your first ninety days look nothing like this, ask why.

Weeks 1–4 — establish the baseline. Rebuild the trailing twelve months into something you can trust. Find where revenue is actually coming from and what it costs to serve. Map the cash cycle. Most founders learn something uncomfortable in this phase, usually about a customer segment or a product line that has been quietly subsidised by the rest.

Weeks 5–8 — build the forward view. A thirteen-week cash forecast, and an annual model with the two or three assumptions that actually drive the outcome isolated so you can flex them. Not a fifty-tab masterpiece. Something you will still be using in month nine.

Weeks 9–12 — install the operating rhythm. A monthly close that lands on a date. A reporting pack that goes to the same people on the same day. A short list of decisions the numbers are now good enough to support: pricing, hiring sequence, whether to raise, what to say to the bank.

Ongoing. Board and lender relationships. Capital planning. Margin work. Being the person in the room who has seen this problem at four other companies.

The close calendar is the part most engagements skip and most regret. We covered the mechanics in Month-End Close in 5 Days.

What it costs

Fractional CFO pricing has become transparent enough to plan around.

Go Fractional's rolling benchmark, drawn from live engagements and public postings, reports a median rate of $175 an hour, an average of $174, and an interquartile range of $125 to $220. Retainers are usually quoted monthly rather than hourly, but the underlying hourly maths is what a retainer is built from.

ArrangementAnnual cost (approx.)What you are buying
Fractional CFO, 5 hrs/week$31,000 – $55,000Review, oversight, board and lender support
Fractional CFO, 10 hrs/week$63,000 – $110,000The above plus active forecasting and planning
Fractional CFO, 20 hrs/week$125,000 – $220,000Near-continuous involvement; approaching full-time economics
Full-time CFO$195,500 – $321,750 base (Robert Half, 2026)Everything, permanently — plus recruiting cost and ramp time

Fractional figures are calculated at 50 working weeks against the Go Fractional 25th–75th percentile range. They exclude any accounting or analyst capacity beneath the role.

That last line is the one worth reading twice. A fractional CFO rate buys the CFO's hours and nothing else. If the close is not clean, if there is no analyst to build the model, if nobody owns the reconciliation, that work either does not happen or it happens at $175 an hour. Both outcomes are bad.

When you actually need one

Revenue is a weak trigger on its own. Two companies at $6M can have very different finance needs depending on inventory, contract structure and how they are funded. The reliable triggers are events and symptoms.

Hire one when:

You are raising, selling, or borrowing in the next twelve months, and your numbers have never been examined by someone hostile.
You are making a decision you cannot model — a pricing change, a new location, a large hire, an acquisition.
Gross margin moved and nobody can explain why.
You are profitable on the P&L and short of cash, repeatedly.
Your board or lender has started asking for things you cannot produce on request.
You spend more than a few hours a week personally reconciling things.

Do not hire one when:

The books are the problem. Fix the accounting first, or fix both at once with the right people on each. A CFO cannot forecast off a ledger nobody trusts.
You want someone to do the work. That is an analyst or a controller. A CFO at eight hours a week will not build your model.
You are pre-revenue and the entire question is whether the product works. Get a good bookkeeper and a simple runway sheet.

The staging question is covered in more depth in The CFO Question Every Founder Gets Wrong at $3M Revenue, and the underlying layers in The Finance Pyramid™.

What a fractional CFO will not fix

This is where most of the disappointment sits, so it is worth being direct.

A fractional CFO supplies judgment. Judgment produces decisions and a list of things to build. Somebody then has to build them, month after month, on a calendar. If that capacity does not exist, the engagement degrades into a series of well-informed meetings about work that is not happening.

You can see it in the pattern. Month one is energising. Month two produces a clear diagnosis. By month five the same three items are still open, the CFO is politely frustrated, and the founder is quietly wondering what they are paying for.

The fix is unglamorous. Pair the fractional CFO with the execution capacity underneath — closing, reconciliation, reporting, model maintenance — and price it as one thing. That is the argument in Why the Best Fractional CFOs Refuse to Work Without an Execution Layer, and it is the reason we built CFOLogic's Full-Stack Finance model the way we did.

There is a second thing a fractional CFO will not fix, and it is harder to say. If the business does not work, better reporting will tell you sooner and in more detail. CB Insights, reviewing 431 venture-backed shutdowns since 2023, found running out of capital cited in 70% of cases — but poor product-market fit in 43%, bad timing in 29%, and unsustainable unit economics in 19%. Running out of money is where the story ends. It is rarely where it starts. A CFO can shorten the time it takes you to find that out, which is worth a great deal. It is not the same as fixing it.

How to evaluate one

Six questions worth asking before you sign. The answers separate the market quickly.

Who does the work you recommend? If the answer is "you have a team, right?" and you do not, keep looking.
How many clients do you carry? Two or three is workable. Six means you are competing for a calendar slot.
What does month three look like? A specific answer means they have done this before. A vague one means you are the pilot.
Have you been through this event? A first raise, a first audit, a first bank covenant, a first integration. Adjacent experience is not the same experience.
What happens if you are unavailable? Illness, holiday, a client crisis elsewhere. A single person is a single point of failure.
How do you leave? Good engagements have an exit — a full-time hire, a documented handover, a defined end. Ask what theirs looks like.

Ask for two references from engagements that ended, not just ones that are running. The ended ones tell you more.

Frequently asked questions

What does a fractional CFO cost?

Market benchmarks put the median rate at $175 an hour, with most engagements between $125 and $220 (Go Fractional, 2026). At ten hours a week that works out to roughly $63,000 to $110,000 a year, against a full-time CFO base salary range of $195,500 to $321,750 (Robert Half, 2026). The fractional rate covers the CFO's time only, not accounting or analyst capacity.

What is the difference between a fractional CFO and an interim CFO?

Fractional is a permanent arrangement at part-time hours. Interim is a full-time seat filled for a fixed period, usually after a departure or during a transaction. Interim engagements are shorter and cost considerably more per month.

At what revenue should a company hire a fractional CFO?

There is no reliable revenue threshold. Most companies find the role earns its cost somewhere between $3M and $10M, but the trigger is an event — a raise, a covenant, a margin problem, an acquisition — rather than a number on the top line.

Can a fractional CFO replace an accountant or bookkeeper?

No. They sit above that work and depend on it. A fractional CFO working from an unreliable ledger will spend the engagement fixing accounting at CFO rates, which is the most expensive way to buy bookkeeping.

How long does a fractional CFO engagement last?

Commonly twelve to thirty-six months. Shorter than that usually means the scope was a project. Longer usually means the arrangement has become the permanent finance function, which is fine as long as everyone has said so out loud.

Is a fractional CFO worth it for a small business?

It depends on whether your problems are decisions or data. If you cannot get accurate numbers, buy accounting capacity first. If you have accurate numbers and cannot decide what to do with them, that is what a CFO is for.
